# MamaMed Prenatal Companion

A maternal health follow up app for expecting mothers and new parents, paired with a clinic dashboard.

## Problem

In many African countries, maternal and newborn mortality remain high because mothers miss antenatal visits, forget key danger signs and lose contact with their health worker after delivery. Clinics operate from a paper register and only call a mother when it is already too late. Public campaigns reach millions but cannot personalize the message.

MamaMed stays with a mother from early pregnancy through the first two years of a child's life, sends timely reminders by SMS and WhatsApp, and lets the clinic see who to follow up.

## Target users

Primary users are expecting and new mothers in urban and peri-urban areas. Secondary users are community health workers and midwives. Tertiary users are maternal health NGOs that run programs across multiple clinics.

## MVP features

1. Opt in patient record. Phone, estimated due date or child date of birth, clinic.
2. Visit schedule. Auto generated antenatal, postnatal and immunization visit calendar.
3. Reminders. SMS and WhatsApp reminders 48 and 24 hours before each visit.
4. Danger sign checker. A short self check for warning signs with a call to action to go to the clinic.
5. Clinic follow up list. Daily list of mothers who missed a visit, with contact ready.
6. Education cards. Short reviewed cards on nutrition, danger signs and infant care, available in English, French and one local language.
7. Simple aggregate report for the clinic and NGO.

## Post MVP ideas

Growth tracking charts, telemedicine bridge to DocDirect, community health worker mobile companion, voice notes in local languages, offline capture, integration with national MCH register where available.

## User stories

As an expecting mother I want a reminder before every clinic visit so that I never miss one. As a midwife I want a daily list of mothers who missed yesterday so that I can call them. As an NGO officer I want to know what percent of my 4000 mothers attended their third ANC visit.

## Success metrics

Enrolled mothers, visit attendance rate, reminder opt out rate, clinic follow ups completed, retention through the full program.

## Revenue model

Per clinic subscription. NGO licensing for program deployments with a reporting dashboard. Sponsored content from maternal health brands is possible but should be reviewed and labeled.

## Risks and compliance

Protected health information must be encrypted at rest and in transit. Clinical content must be reviewed by a qualified health professional and sourced from WHO, local ministry of health or a reputable NGO. Never diagnose a user. Always recommend a visit to a qualified professional. Be careful with gender identity assumptions and avoid shaming language.

## Research references

WHO maternal health guidelines, national MCH handbooks from Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana and Rwanda, PATH mobile health reports, MomConnect in South Africa as an inspiration.

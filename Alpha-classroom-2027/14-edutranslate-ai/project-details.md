# EduTranslate AI

An AI assisted translator and simplifier that turns English or French learning content into local African languages for teachers and parents.

## Problem

Across the continent, lesson plans and official content are published in English or French, while millions of learners are more fluent in Swahili, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Amharic, Wolof, Zulu or Twi. Teachers have to translate on the fly, which is slow and inconsistent. Parents cannot help a child with homework when the material is in a language they do not read well.

EduTranslate lets a teacher or parent paste a lesson or upload a photo of a textbook page, translate it into a chosen local language, simplify the wording to a grade level and listen to it being read aloud.

## Target users

Primary users are primary and secondary teachers. Secondary users are parents and guardians. Tertiary users are NGOs and tutoring centers that produce multilingual materials.

## MVP features

1. Input. Paste text, upload image with OCR, or dictate.
2. Language selection. Source and target, with five local languages for the first release.
3. Translation. AI assisted, with a confidence score.
4. Simplification. Grade level selector (primary 3 to 8).
5. Read aloud. Audio playback and download.
6. Lesson library. Save and share with a class link.
7. Correction feedback. User can flag a bad translation. Flags feed into a review queue.

## Post MVP ideas

Textbook batch import, printable worksheet export, native speaker review network for quality, parent companion mode, deep integration with ExamPrep AI.

## User stories

As a teacher I want to send a short lesson summary in Swahili to parents of class 4. As a parent I want to hear a homework question in Yoruba. As an NGO I want to translate 200 health education cards into Hausa at low cost.

## Success metrics

Lessons translated per week, read aloud plays, class share clicks, user corrections submitted, teacher retention.

## Revenue model

Teacher subscription, school bulk license, NGO licensing for program content.

## Risks and compliance

AI translations can be wrong, especially for cultural idioms. Mark translations as AI assisted. Encourage native speaker correction. Never present a translation as official curriculum. Store user data minimally.

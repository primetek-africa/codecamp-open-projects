# SafeSOS Panic Button

A personal panic button and trusted contact alert app for women, students and night shift workers.

## Problem

Street crime, domestic abuse and workplace harassment are everyday concerns across African cities. Many victims cannot reach emergency services quickly, and when they do, response times are long and inconsistent. People want a fast way to raise an alarm with someone who will actually come, which is usually a family member or a close friend.

SafeSOS gives a user a one tap panic button that alerts trusted contacts with location and optional audio, logs the incident and helps community organizations respond.

## Target users

Primary users are women across urban Africa, university students, delivery riders and night shift workers. Secondary users are employers who want to protect staff. Tertiary users are community based organizations that respond to local emergencies.

## MVP features

1. Account setup. Phone verification, trusted contacts opt in.
2. One tap panic. Sends SMS, WhatsApp and push to trusted contacts with location.
3. Short audio snippet. 30 seconds recorded and sent after a panic.
4. Incident log. Private record of each event.
5. Community safe zone map. Verified safe spaces like 24 hour pharmacies, hospitals and police posts near the user.
6. False alarm cancel within a short window.
7. Battery friendly background mode.

## Post MVP ideas

Employer dashboards, insurance integration, campus specific builds, direct to local emergency number where supported, group alerts, panic via volume button pattern.

## User stories

As a woman walking home at night I want to send a panic alert with one tap. As a parent I want to know my daughter triggered an alert and where she is. As a university safety officer I want aggregated data about where incidents happen.

## Success metrics

Verified users, trusted contacts added per user, panic alerts delivered, false alarm rate, user retention.

## Revenue model

Freemium. Family and employer paid plans for group management.

## Risks and compliance

Audio capture requires clear consent and should be one time per panic. Keep incident data private to the user. Do not share phone numbers outside trusted contacts. Make clear this is not a replacement for emergency services.
